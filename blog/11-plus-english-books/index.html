11 Plus English Books: Which Ones Are Worth Buying

10 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Match your book choice to the target school's exam board: GL Assessment suits Bond or CGP GL-style papers, while independent school entry needs written comprehension and creative writing practice.
  • Choose CGP to teach a Year 4 starter and Bond for harder, exam-standard timed papers in Year 5.
  • Diagnose errors by skill, not score: split wrong answers into comprehension, vocabulary, technique and timing buckets before buying anything.
  • One core workbook plus one book for the diagnosed weak skill is enough; a shelf of part-finished books wastes money and hides progress.
  • Move from skills books in Year 4 to one timed paper a week from spring of Year 5, and consider a tutor only if three workbooks in a row produce the same errors.

The right 11 plus English books depend on your target school's exam board and your child's weakest skill. GL grammar schools suit Bond or CGP GL-style papers, and independent school entry needs harder comprehension and creative writing. Most children need one focused workbook per gap rather than a shelf of them. This guide matches each major series to those two factors so you buy once, not five times.

Which 11 plus English books match GL vs independent school exam formats?

The exam your target school uses decides the book. GL Assessment papers are multiple-choice with strict timing; independent school papers are usually written answers with comprehension and a piece of creative writing. Buying a comprehension-and-essay workbook for a multiple-choice paper wastes months, and the reverse leaves writing skills untouched. Check your school's admissions page first: Sutton, Barnet and Tiffin all publish which board sets their English paper.

What GL Assessment English papers ask for

GL English tests comprehension, grammar, punctuation, spelling and vocabulary, almost always in multiple-choice format. That favours books with short, timed question sets and answer sheets to mark against. Bond and CGP both publish GL-style practice papers. Their vocabulary-heavy word lists matter because GL comprehension passages lean on wide vocabulary.

What independent school English papers ask for

Independent schools set their own papers, typically a written comprehension plus a story or descriptive piece. Here you need full-text comprehension workbooks and a dedicated writing guide, and past papers from the school itself beat any series.

| Exam type | What the paper looks like | Book that fits | |---|---|---| | GL Assessment | Multiple-choice, timed, vocabulary-heavy | Bond or CGP GL practice papers | | Independent school | Written comprehension plus creative writing | Full-answer comprehension workbooks plus a writing guide | | CEM-style | Mixed-format, broad vocabulary, no published papers | Vocabulary builders and varied mixed practice |

When is Bond 11 plus English the right choice versus CGP?

Choose Bond if your child is already competent at comprehension and needs harder papers closer to exam standard. Choose CGP if your child is starting in Year 4, needs friendlier explanations, or you want cheap targeted practice on one weak skill. Many families use CGP first, then move to Bond.

Bond 11 plus English: strengths and gaps

Bond's English books are the closest thing to the real thing for GL Assessment-style grammar school papers. The comprehension passages are longer and drier than a child would choose, the vocabulary is pitched high, and the question styles mirror what selective schools actually ask. That difficulty is the point: a child scoring comfortably on Bond Assessment Papers at 10-11 is genuinely ready.

The gaps are as real as the strengths. Bond explains very little. If your child cannot yet tell you what inference means, or why an author chose a particular word, a Bond book will mark them wrong without teaching them why. Bond also assumes a baseline of reading stamina that reluctant readers often lack, so it can dent confidence if introduced too early.

CGP 11 plus English: strengths and gaps

CGP does the opposite well. Its books teach first and test second, with plain-English explanations, worked examples, and a lighter tone that suits a Year 4 child starting out. The single-topic books (spelling, punctuation, comprehension) let you buy only what your child struggles with, which keeps costs down.

The trade-off is ceiling. CGP practice tends to sit below the difficulty of real GL papers, so a child who only ever works through CGP can feel well prepared at home and be surprised in the exam room. Treat CGP as the teaching layer and Bond as the testing layer rather than picking one and stopping there.

What should an 11 plus English book cover to match the syllabus?

Before buying, check the book against three areas: comprehension, grammar and punctuation, and timed writing. Most 11 plus English papers draw on these, whether the exam is GL Assessment format or a school's own paper.

Comprehension

Look for a fiction extract followed by 10-15 questions that mix retrieval (find the fact) with inference (why did the character do that?). Books should label question types so you can spot weak areas. Both CGP and Bond publish 11+ English ranges that list the skills each book targets and the age range, so read the product page before you buy.

Grammar and punctuation

Your child needs to identify word classes (noun, verb, adjective), spot subordinate clauses, and use apostrophes and semicolons correctly. GL-style papers often test this as multiple choice.

Creative and timed writing

Independent school papers usually ask for a story or description in 25-30 minutes. A good book gives prompts plus worked examples, not just titles.

How do you diagnose which English skill your child needs to revise?

Before buying another book, run one past paper or timed practice test and mark it for skill, not score. Split every wrong answer into four buckets: comprehension (missed the meaning), vocabulary (didn't know a word), technique (couldn't answer the question type), or timing (ran out of time). A 60% score tells you nothing; "8 of 12 errors were vocabulary" tells you exactly which book to buy.

Marking for skill, not score

Go through the paper with a highlighter and label each error with one of the four buckets. Count them. If timing errors dominate, the fix is practice under a clock, not a harder book. If comprehension dominates, look at whether your child is skimming rather than reading the passage properly.

Common skill-gap patterns

Two patterns cover most Year 4/5 children. Strong readers with weak vocabulary need word-list and synonym work before more comprehension passages. Confident readers who rush need timed papers to build pace, because accuracy drops when the clock is unfamiliar. Re-run the same diagnostic after four to six weeks to check the gap actually closed.

How many 11 plus English practice books does a child actually need?

One core workbook plus one question bank for the weakest skill is enough for most Year 4 and 5 children. A shelf of books usually means the child does a few pages of each and finishes none, and you cannot see which skill actually improved.

Pick one series matched to your target exam format, work it cover to cover, then add a second book only for the diagnosed weak area, such as comprehension or verbal reasoning. A stack of part-finished books costs more than one finished one.

The same logic applies to subscriptions: paid 11+ platforms charge monthly, so adding an unused platform to a pile of unused books compounds waste. Finish what you own first.

What order should you work through 11 plus English revision books?

Work skills books before timed papers. A Year 4 child who jumps straight into past papers usually learns to guess; sequencing fixes that.

Year 4: build the skills

Year 4 is for building the underlying skills, not for exam practice. Start with a skills-building book covering comprehension, grammar, punctuation and vocabulary, and keep sessions short: ten to fifteen minutes, one sitting a day. Bond and CGP both publish skills-level titles suited to this stage, so pick whichever tone your child responds to. Aim for accuracy over speed at every step; if a comprehension answer comes back wrong, sit down and work out why before moving on, because an uncorrected misconception compounds.

Add vocabulary steadily alongside the workbook, since both verbal reasoning and comprehension reward a wide word bank, and a few new words a week is enough. Resist the temptation to introduce timed papers this early. A Year 4 child who meets past papers too soon learns to guess rather than read. That habit is far harder to unlearn in Year 5 than it was to avoid.

Year 5: timed papers and mocks

From the spring of Year 5, switch to full papers under a clock, matched to your target school's board, GL Assessment or independent format. One timed paper a week is enough; mark it together and log weak topics. Spend the following week's sessions on those gaps, then test again. In the final month, use papers as dress rehearsals at the real exam time of day.

When do you need tuition instead of more workbooks?

A tutor earns their £25-45/hr when a workbook keeps failing. Perhaps your child scores well on topics they like but stalls on comprehension or timed writing, or will not open a book with you at all. A tutor diagnoses the specific weak skill, sets work accordingly, and provides the accountability a parent policing practice cannot.

What tuition fixes that books cannot

Books are static. They cannot spot that your child misreads the question stem, loses marks on inference, or runs out of time on long comprehension. If three workbooks in a row produce the same errors, that pattern is what a tutor is for. Books also cannot make a reluctant child practise daily; if getting your child to sit down and work is the bottleneck, that is exactly the gap a tutor fills.

Middle-cost options between books and tutors

Before paying hourly rates, try structured online practice. Bond 11+ Online and EdPlace offer marked practice at a fraction of tuition cost, and our own Sagito adds a daily question bank at £3.99/mo. Honest limitation: none of these diagnose deep comprehension gaps the way a human does. If a month of app practice shows no movement on the same weak skill, book the tutor.

A workbook is a one-off cost and collects no data. Atom Learning and Bond 11+ Online charge monthly subscriptions, so a year can cost more than a shelf of Bond and CGP books. Tuition at £25-45/hr adds up fastest. The real question is which one your child opens without you standing over them.

FAQ

Are Bond or CGP 11 plus English books enough on their own to pass the 11+?

Usually yes, if you pick the series matched to your target exam format and finish it properly. CGP teaches but sits below real GL paper difficulty, so pair it with Bond-style timed papers before the exam. For independent school entry, add past papers from the school itself, since no commercial series fully replicates a written comprehension plus creative writing format.

Which 11 plus English book suits a child starting in Year 4?

Start with CGP. Its books explain concepts in plain English before testing, the tone suits a nine-year-old, and the single-topic titles let you buy only the skill your child struggles with. Keep sessions to ten or fifteen minutes and prioritise accuracy over speed. Save Bond for Year 5, when harder, exam-standard papers help rather than dent confidence.

Do grammar schools in Sutton and Barnet use GL or CEM for English?

Check each school's admissions page rather than assuming. Both boroughs publish which board sets their English papers, and boards change, so a forum answer from two years ago is unreliable. GL Assessment describes its own test format on its website. Once you confirm the board, buy practice papers matched to that format instead of generic 11+ books.

Is the 11 plus English test harder than SATs?

Yes, in depth and pace. SATs test Year 6 curriculum skills; 11 plus papers push vocabulary well beyond that, demand inference rather than retrieval, and compress everything into strict timing. A child comfortably hitting the expected standard at school can still struggle with GL-style comprehension passages, which is why a diagnostic practice paper is worth running before you buy anything.

Can I use free 11 plus English papers instead of buying books?

Partly. Some schools and publishers release free sample papers, and these are useful as diagnostics to identify whether errors come from comprehension, vocabulary, technique or timing. But free papers rarely teach: they mark answers wrong without explaining why. For a child with a skill gap, a workbook that explains first and tests second earns its price.
