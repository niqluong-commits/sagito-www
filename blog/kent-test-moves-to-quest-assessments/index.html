The Kent Test moves to Quest Assessments in 2027

25 November 2026 · Sagito

If your child is in Year 5 in Kent, you may have seen that the Kent Test is changing provider. This article sets out what the council has actually said, what is still unknown, and what is sensible to do while we wait for more detail.

What has been announced

In October 2026 Kent County Council said it had appointed a new provider, Quest Assessments, for the Kent Test from 2027. The council says that the test’s purpose, format, timing and role in grammar school admissions will remain the same. It also says there will be no changes to the admissions process, eligibility criteria, support arrangements or Headteacher Assessments, and that families should see very little difference.

The first test set by the new provider is the one sat in September 2027. Children take the Kent Test in September at the start of Year 6, so this is the test for children who are in Year 5 now. It is the route to a Year 7 place in September 2028. Children in Year 6 this year sit the current test, in September 2026, which has already taken place.

What stays the same

According to the council’s preparation page, the structure is:

  • Paper one: English and maths. About an hour. Each section has a five-minute practice exercise followed by a 25-minute test.
  • Paper two: reasoning. About an hour, with a verbal reasoning section and a non-verbal reasoning section of roughly equal length. The non-verbal part is split into short sections, each timed on its own.
  • A writing exercise. It is not marked, but a local headteacher panel may use it at the Headteacher Assessment stage. It lasts 40 minutes, including 10 minutes of planning.
  • Multiple choice, marked by computer, with a separate answer sheet.

What we do not know yet

  • Familiarisation materials. The council says new materials will be available “when we have them”. Quest and the council have published a joint information page for families, with a familiarisation booklet and guidance for parents and children, which are worth a look.
  • Dates for 2028 entry. At the time of writing we have not seen registration, test or results dates for September 2027. Registration is made through the council, not through schools, so watch the council’s page.
  • The qualifying score. The council sets the standard after each test. Nobody outside the council can tell you in advance what it will be.
  • How questions will look. Some tutoring websites predict a change in style. Those are guesses, not announcements, and the council’s own wording is that very little will differ.

What families can do now

Nothing about this change calls for a new approach. The same steady habits apply: regular reading, secure number work, and short, relaxed practice with the kinds of question children meet in school. Quest’s own guidance for parents says the test covers what children have learned up to the end of Year 5, so there is no need to race ahead into Year 6 content.

Keep an eye on the council’s page for dates, and keep practice short and calm.
