What is on the Kent Test papers?

26 November 2026 · Sagito

The Kent Test looks different from a school test because it is all multiple choice and timed. Knowing the layout in advance takes away a lot of the surprise. This guide describes the papers using the council’s and Quest Assessments’ published information.

Two papers, one session

Everyone sits the same papers, whichever Kent grammar school they hope to attend. Both papers are taken in the same session, with a short supervised break between them. Each child gets a question booklet and a separate answer sheet for each paper.

Paper one: English and maths

About an hour in total. Each section starts with a five-minute practice exercise, then a 25-minute test.

English includes a reading comprehension and further questions on vocabulary, spelling, punctuation and grammar. Quest’s familiarisation booklet shows questions such as finding a word with the same meaning as another, choosing the evidence in a passage that supports a statement, and choosing the correctly punctuated sentence.

Maths mixes quick arithmetic with problems that take several steps. Quest describes some questions as mental arithmetic with little working and others as multi-step reasoning. The familiarisation booklet includes missing-number sums, division, factors, area of a combined shape and a percentage problem set out as a part-whole model.

Paper two: reasoning

About an hour, with practice questions before each section.

Verbal reasoning is about 20 minutes. It tests word meaning and logic: pairing words that mean the same, joining two words to make a new one, completing a sentence with the right word, finding the odd ones out, and working out an answer from a short set of facts.

Non-verbal and spatial reasoning is split into short sections of four to five minutes each. It covers sequences, completing grids, reflections, how a folded and cut piece of paper looks when opened out, and which cube a net would make.

Some questions need more than one answer

Several sample questions ask for two answers or for all the answers that apply. It is a small thing, but it catches children out. Teach them to read the instruction line first: “choose two” means exactly that.

The writing exercise

There is also a 40-minute writing task, with 10 minutes for planning. It is not marked, but it may be read by a headteacher panel later in the process, so it is worth a child writing as clearly as they can.

Where to find the real examples

Quest publishes a free familiarisation booklet, linked from its Kent Test page for families, with sample questions from each section and an answer key. Working through it together, at a relaxed pace, is the most direct way to see what the papers look like.
